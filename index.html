From a single brush stroke to a van Gogh. Each work is a picture to copy with a robot's brush or pen, stroke by stroke, the way a person would.

In the studio

The reference robot at work, ray traced. Each film replays a graded run, and its last sheet is that run's, pixel for pixel.

The collection

Five disciplines, each with its own brush. Hover a work for the reference robot's copy; open it to compare and to watch it made.

Written, not coloured in

A fine tip can trace round and round a character until it is filled, and the picture alone would pass. RoboPaint also judges the hand: brush strokes, made as a calligrapher or a painter makes them.

How a work is judged

Aligned first, then compared. Writing is judged by its ink, painting by its colour; both by their strokes.

Open book, closed book

Every work is set twice. In the studio the agent may look at the exact sheet, ask for its score and start over; on stage it sees only what a camera sees, and paint is permanent.

Results

Two code agents, every work, both modes. A point is a work solved: the picture matches and the hand passes.

At the easel

Every copy the two agents handed in, hung beside its exemplar. A red seal marks a work solved; a note says what failed, the picture or the hand. Open one to compare it with the exemplar.

RoboPaint

Brushwork for code agents: a suite of the Embodied First Exam. Paper, code and data links will follow.

Made with ManiSkill 3 and SAPIEN (the robot), libmypaint and mypaint-brushes (paint), a re-implementation of Wang et al.'s dynamic brush (IROS 2020, calligraphy), a broad-edge and a pointed-pen nib model of our own.
Exemplars AR PL UKai via Make Me a Hanzi (Arphic Public License), public-domain rubbings and paintings from Wikimedia Commons, lettering and acrylic exemplars made for RoboPaint (CC0). Robot: Franka Emika Panda (meshes Apache-2.0).
